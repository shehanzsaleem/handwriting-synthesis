question: |
  A capillary bed has a total cross-sectional area 600 times greater than that of the single artery supplying it.
  Blood flows through the supplying artery at a mean velocity of 300 mm per second. Using the principle that
  velocity × cross-sectional area is constant, calculate the mean blood velocity in the capillaries, in mm per
  second, and explain the functional significance of this change in velocity for exchange at the capillary.
---
## Q5

$$ v_{\text{artery}} \times A_{\text{artery}} = v_{\text{cap}} \times A_{\text{cap}} $$ {M1}

$$ v_{\text{cap}} = \frac{300}{600} $$ {M1}

$$ \boxed{v_{\text{cap}} = 0.5 \text{ mm per second}} $$ {A1}

The total cross-sectional area is much larger, so the blood moves much more slowly through each capillary. {B1}

This gives a longer transit time, so there is more time for diffusion of oxygen, glucose and carbon dioxide between the blood and the tissue fluid around the cells. {B1}

So exchange is most efficient exactly where it is needed, at the capillaries. {B1}
