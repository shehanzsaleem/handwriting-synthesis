question: |
  A single enantiomer of 2-bromobutane is hydrolysed with aqueous sodium hydroxide. Explain, with reference to the
  mechanism, why the product is a racemic mixture when the reaction proceeds by SN1, but a single (inverted)
  enantiomer when it proceeds by SN2.

## Q6

**SN2**: one step. The hydroxide ion attacks the carbon from the side opposite the bromine (backside attack) as the C-Br bond breaks. {B1}

So the configuration at the chiral centre is inverted, and only one enantiomer forms. {B1}

**SN1**: the C-Br bond breaks first, giving a carbocation intermediate. {B1}

The carbocation is trigonal planar at the central carbon. {B1}

The hydroxide ion can attack either face with equal probability, so equal amounts of both enantiomers form: a racemic mixture. {B1 B1}
