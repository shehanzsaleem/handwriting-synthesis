question: |
  The rate of an enzyme-catalysed reaction is 12.0 micromoles per minute with no inhibitor, and 7.5 with an inhibitor
  present. When the substrate concentration is doubled, with the same inhibitor concentration, the rate rises to 10.8.
  Calculate the percentage decrease in rate caused by the inhibitor, and the percentage of the uninhibited rate restored
  by doubling the substrate concentration. Use these results to justify whether the inhibitor is competitive or
  non-competitive.
---
## 4 (b)

$$ \text{decrease} = \frac{12.0 - 7.5}{12.0} \times 100 $$ {M1}

$$ = 37.5\% $$ {A1}

$$ \frac{10.8}{12.0} \times 100 = 90\% \text{ of the uninhibited rate} $$ {M1 A1}

Doubling the substrate brings the rate most of the way back to the uninhibited rate. {B1}

So the inhibitor is competitive: it binds to the active site, and more substrate molecules outcompete it for the active site. A non-competitive inhibitor binds elsewhere and changes the shape of the active site, so adding substrate could not overcome it. {B1}
